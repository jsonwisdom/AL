# VERIFIER_SERVICE_CONTRACT_V1

Status: DRAFT_LOCKED_FOR_REVIEW

## 1. Purpose

This contract defines the economic membrane for paid verification work performed by Jay / JSONWisdom / ALMS operators.

Payment buys process access. Payment does not buy truth.

## 2. Core doctrine

> Pay Jay for work. Do not sell truth.

A payer may compensate the operator for labor, ceremony support, replay execution, infrastructure maintenance, transcript generation, and verifier operations.

A payer may not purchase, influence, require, or negotiate a favorable verdict.

## 3. Service scope

Covered services may include:

- replay preparation;
- schema review;
- payload canonicalization;
- receipt generation;
- verifier execution;
- transcript emission;
- ENS / Git / EAS pointer review;
- public verification support;
- implementation guidance.

## 4. Non-influence covenant

The payer acknowledges:

- verifier exit code controls admissibility;
- a paid run may PASS, FAIL, REFUSE, or return INDETERMINATE;
- payment does not alter exit codes;
- payment does not relax invariants;
- payment does not authorize private transforms;
- payment does not override schema, Git, EAS, ENS, or hash evidence.

## 5. Refusal conditions

The operator must refuse or halt work if any of the following occur:

- requested outcome manipulation;
- request to hide evidence or mismatches;
- request to reuse testnet identifiers as mainnet identifiers;
- request to bypass canonical hashing;
- request to skip receipt generation;
- request to assert a verdict without replay;
- request that creates a ghost anchor;
- request that conflicts with the governing schema or verifier rules.

## 6. Deliverables

Deliverables may include:

- verifier output;
- replay log;
- receipt JSON;
- transcript JSON;
- Git commit reference;
- EAS UID / transaction reference;
- ENS text-record map;
- failure report with deterministic reason.

Deliverables are procedural artifacts, not guaranteed favorable outcomes.

## 7. SLA and timing

Unless separately agreed in writing:

- replay jobs are best-effort;
- no uptime guarantee is implied;
- urgent work may require separate fee terms;
- external network failures, RPC downtime, GitHub downtime, EAS downtime, ENS resolver downtime, and wallet failures are outside operator control.

## 8. Confidentiality and public evidence

Verification systems prefer public evidence.

If private materials are supplied, they must be clearly marked. Private inputs may produce non-public work products, but public admissibility requires public replayable evidence unless a sealed-evidence process is separately defined.

## 9. Payment separation

Payment receipts must be distinct from governance receipts.

A payment transaction is evidence of compensation only. It is not evidence of truth, admissibility, validity, or correctness.

## 10. No legal conclusion

Verifier outputs are technical-admissibility artifacts. They are not legal advice, legal conclusions, court orders, financial advice, or official government determinations.

## 11. Payer affirmation

By requesting paid verification services, the payer affirms:

- I am paying for process, not outcome.
- I will not represent payment as proof of truth.
- I understand a paid verification may fail.
- I accept that verifier output and replay evidence control the verdict.

## 12. Operator affirmation

The operator affirms:

- I will not sell favorable verdicts.
- I will not alter invariants for payment.
- I will preserve receipt boundaries.
- I will disclose deterministic failure reasons when available.
- I will keep TRACK_001-style governance membranes intact.
