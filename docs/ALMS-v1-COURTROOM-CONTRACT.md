## 1. Purpose

This surface defines the constitutional contract for the ALMS-v1 Courtroom.

The Courtroom is not a sovereign interpreter of reality.

The Courtroom is an institutional admissibility layer downstream of mechanically sovereign evidence.

The Courtroom governs what may be treated as admissible for institutional action. It does not govern truth itself.

## 2. Opening Prohibitions

The Courtroom begins with prohibitions, not powers.

```text
COURTROOM DOES NOT:
- create facts
- rewrite provenance
- manufacture reconstructability
- reinterpret replay outcomes
- alter execution seals
- override refusal states
- normalize broken equivalence
```

These prohibitions are constitutional locks. They are not implementation guidance.

Any courtroom act that violates these prohibitions is void under ALMS-v1.

## 3. Sovereignty Ordering

ALMS-v1 uses a one-directional sovereignty lattice:

```text
Reality
  ↓
Provenance
  ↓
Replay
  ↓
Execution
  ↓
Refusal Boundaries
  ↓
Courtroom
  ↓
Institutional Action
```

This is not a workflow.

This is constitutional ordering.

Each layer inherits constraints from upstream layers. No downstream layer may reinterpret the sovereignty conditions of an upstream layer.

## 4. Mechanical Sovereignty Is Upstream

The following layers are mechanically sovereign relative to the Courtroom:

- Provenance: declarative sovereignty
- Replay: mechanical sovereignty
- Execution: computational sovereignty
- Equivalence: executable comparability sovereignty
- Refusal: boundary sovereignty

The Courtroom inherits their outputs and refusal states.

It does not renegotiate them.

## 5. Courtroom Mandate

The Courtroom may govern only:

- admissibility
- taint
- standing
- jurisdiction
- institutional consequence classification
- verdict vocabulary

The Courtroom may not govern:

- ontology
- reality
- lineage
- reconstructability
- execution integrity
- equivalence law
- sealed computation history

The Courtroom is an institutional sovereignty layer, not a mechanical sovereignty layer.

## 6. Humility Invariant

Reality remains outside machine jurisdiction.

```text
machine output != reality
machine authority != truth
courtroom verdict != fact creation
```

The machine may reconstruct claims, seal computation, classify admissibility, enumerate uncertainty, and refuse overreach.

The machine may never claim sovereign ownership over reality itself.

## 7. Power to Stop

A lawful Courtroom may terminate without synthetic closure.

A Courtroom may issue a refusal or non-admissibility classification when any of the following conditions are present:

- unresolved replay conflict
- reconstructability unavailable
- tainted provenance
- insufficient standing
- inadmissible evidence
- jurisdiction exceeded
- execution seal unavailable
- equivalence failure unresolved

The Courtroom MUST NOT invent continuity to preserve institutional action.

Constitutional integrity is mandatory.

Institutional continuity is not.

## 8. Verdict Vocabulary

The canonical ALMS-v1 Courtroom verdict vocabulary is:

```text
ADMISSIBLE
INADMISSIBLE
TAINTED
STANDING_DENIED
JURISDICTION_DENIED
REFUSED
NO_VERDICT
```

A verdict is an institutional classification under bounded authority.

A verdict is not a truth claim.

## 9. Taint Handling

Taint is an admissibility condition, not an ontology claim.

A tainted claim may be real, false, incomplete, or materially correct.

The Courtroom does not decide reality by declaring taint.

It decides whether institutional action may rely on the claim under ALMS-v1.

## 10. Courtroom Refusal Conditions

The Courtroom MUST refuse when:

- replay is not reconstructable
- execution is not sealed
- provenance is inadmissible
- standing is absent or invalid
- jurisdiction is exceeded
- upstream refusal states are unresolved
- equivalence is broken or undefined
- the requested action would require manufacturing reconstructability

Courtroom refusal is a lawful institutional act.

It is not a runtime failure.

## 11. Non-Recursion Rule

The Courtroom may not create authority for itself by interpreting its own boundaries.

If courtroom jurisdiction is unclear, the Courtroom MUST refuse.

If admissibility requires reinterpretation of upstream mechanical sovereignty, the Courtroom MUST refuse.

No courtroom act may expand courtroom jurisdiction retroactively.

## 12. Constitutional Firewall

The firewall doctrine of ALMS-v1 is:

```text
Mechanical sovereignty constrains institutional sovereignty.
Institutional sovereignty may classify admissibility,
but may never manufacture reconstructability.
```

Any successor epoch may extend Courtroom procedure, but may not retroactively weaken this doctrine for ALMS-v1.
