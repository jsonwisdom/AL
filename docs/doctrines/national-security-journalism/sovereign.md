# SOVEREIGN RECORD — National-Security Journalism Doctrine

**Doctrine ID:** `NSJ-001`  
**Version:** `1.0.0`  
**Authority:** `jsonwisdom/AL`  
**Status:** `RATIFIED`

## SOVEREIGN RECORD

### 1. Controlled-Truth Invariant

The government may possess an authoritative record while the public receives only a managed representation of that record. Journalism SHALL distinguish the underlying event, the government record, retained material, classified material, released material, independently verified material, and the public narrative.

### 2. Authority Is Not Epistemic Proof

Authority to investigate, classify, prosecute, redact, publish, or describe compliance SHALL NOT be treated as proof that a claim is true, complete, or independently verified.

### 3. Two-Records Rule

National-security reporting SHALL distinguish:

- **Sovereign record:** the fuller record held by agencies, prosecutors, intelligence bodies, courts, and protected systems.
- **Civic record:** the material citizens, journalists, counsel, victims, historians, and oversight bodies can inspect.

Officials SHALL NOT describe the civic record as complete unless completeness against the sovereign record has been independently demonstrated.

### 4. Authenticity–Reliability Separation

An authentic document may contain an inaccurate, coerced, contaminated, mistranslated, selectively copied, or otherwise unreliable statement.

Therefore:

- authentic document ≠ accurate statement;
- accurate quotation ≠ reliable allegation;
- classified source ≠ truthful source;
- repeated intelligence ≠ independent corroboration.

### 5. Journalism-Before-Hash Rule

Verification SHALL proceed in this order:

Authority → Provenance → Context → Source reliability → Corroboration → Public-interest analysis → Victim protection → Contradiction testing → Hash.

A hash proves byte identity only. It does not prove authorship, completeness, credibility, voluntariness, lawful withholding, contextual integrity, or public-interest value.

### 6. Negative-Space Rule

Absence alone SHALL NOT be treated as proof of wrongdoing. Repeated and documented patterns of missing attachments, unexplained docket gaps, delayed releases, inconsistent redactions, inaccessible exhibits, retroactive classification, or metadata conflicts MAY become legitimate investigative objects.

### 7. Corpus-Completeness Rule

A large production SHALL NOT be described as complete solely because an authority says it is complete. Corpus claims require documented source systems, custodians, date ranges, collection rules, deduplication rules, attachment handling, OCR failure logs, removal history, replacement history, and stable identifiers.

### 8. Protected-Interest Rule

National-security secrecy may protect legitimate sources, methods, proceedings, and people. Victim protection and lawful classification SHALL be assessed as real constraints, not dismissed automatically and not accepted without traceable authority and review.

### 9. Unknown-State Rule

The following are valid and preferred states when evidence is insufficient:

`UNKNOWN`, `NOT PUBLIC`, `UNVERIFIED`, `INFERRED`, and `CONTESTED`.

Uncertainty SHALL be recorded rather than converted into narrative certainty.

### 10. Receipt Rule

Every consequential national-security claim SHOULD carry a verification envelope identifying source status, document identity, release channel, redactions, corroboration, contradictions, victim-risk review, government response, confidence, unknowns, revision triggers, and artifact hash.

## Canonical Maxim

> National security is sometimes a valid reason for secrecy. It is never a substitute for a receipt showing who invoked it, when, under what authority, and subject to what review.
