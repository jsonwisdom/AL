## Purpose

Define the emergency management scheme for ENS, EAS, parent-child routing, and ALMS replay.

This is a coordination and verification scheme. It is not a legal appointment, custody transfer, medical directive, financial authority, or emergency services substitute.

## Source Roles

- AL: doctrine and scheme source

- JOY: protected family-facing continuity layer

- EAS: attestation witness layer
- ALMS: receipt memory and replay layer

## Parent / Child Structure

Parent means root controller or guardian context for routing.
Child means protected dependent context or delegated sub-route.

No child identity is exposed by default.
No child route is public unless explicitly safe and receipt-bound.
No parent route creates authority without an external qualified process.

## Emergency Management Flow

1. Observe event.
2. Declare emergency context.
3. Protect family identity and custody first.
4. Preserve receipts and continuity second.
5. Route to ENS display if identity discovery is needed.
6. Route to EAS only for attestation of what was claimed, when, and by whom.
7. Route to ALMS for replay memory.
8. Expand public surface only after safety review.

## ENS Rule

ENS names route identity. They do not prove authority.

Allowed ENS fields:
- public root name
- public recovery contact surface if intentionally published
- pointer to ALMS public replay index
- pointer to EAS attestation only after receipt exists

Forbidden ENS claims:
- custody transfer
- legal authority
- medical authority
- emergency services authority
- identity exposure for protected children

## EAS Rule

EAS witnesses claims. It does not make claims true.

Allowed attestations:
- emergency context declared
- receipt hash observed
- public replay index updated
- parent-child route schema version published

Forbidden attestations:
- universal truth claims
- legal custody claims
- medical decision claims
- official agency authority claims
