Due Process at Machine Speed

This is a constitutional framework for machine-speed government decisions.

It is not AI ethics. It is not a technology proposal. It is procedural due process applied to automated public power: when the government uses machines to affect rights, benefits, or eligibility, the decision must be reviewable.

What this means

Core claim: When an agency uses an automated or semi-automated system to make a materially consequential decision, the administrative record must include the computational basis of the decision, not merely the inputs and output.

What this is: A due-process framework for notice, hearing, administrative record, and meaningful review in machine-mediated government decisions.

What this is not: A ban on automation, a demand for public source-code disclosure, or an AI ethics pledge.

The two-track framework

Track 1: Execution fidelity

Did the system do what the record says it did?

  • Was the right policy version used?
  • Were the correct inputs used?
  • Can the decision path be checked?

Track 2: Substantive legitimacy

Was the system allowed to do that at all?

  • Was the rule lawful?
  • Was the threshold fair?
  • Can the person understand and challenge the explanation?
