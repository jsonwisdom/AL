Executive Order on Replayability, Provenance, and Audit Standards

Liberty and property interests protected by the Constitution cannot be delegated to uninspectable processes.

Automated systems that determine or materially influence public outcomes shall not operate beyond meaningful review. When an AI system affects benefits, housing, credit, employment, legal recommendations, public eligibility, or access to essential services, its output must carry a replayable record.

Constitutional Rules

Replay Requirement

Material AI outputs must preserve model ID, policy hash, timestamp, input provenance, transformation chain, and output receipt hash.

Drift Disclosure

Material model changes must be logged. Prior outputs remain historically preserved. Silent model replacement is prohibited.

Public Audit Memory

Covered systems must maintain append-only logs for policy changes, upgrades, overrides, replay failures, and drift events.

Human Review

A score is not a verdict. AI confidence cannot become final authority over protected interests.

Multi-Observer Verification

Consensus ≠ convergence. Agreement without reproducible replay is insufficient.

Allegation Boundary

Systems may classify evidence states. They may not infer criminality, motive, or intent without human adjudication.

Byte by Byte: Architectural Due Process

The Ceiling

The AI Clarity Act limits what the state may delegate to uninspectable automation. It is the negative constitutional constraint: no black-box governance over protected interests.

The Floor

Byte by Byte requires verification to be built into the substrate at the same resolution the system operates. Not after-the-fact summaries. Replayable records.

ARCHITECTURAL_DUE_PROCESS:

  unless the underlying computation is verifiable byte by byte
  by independent observers without proprietary permission.

Structural Guarantees

  • Verifiable computation: Outputs carry replay receipts, not decorative metadata.
  • Temporal fidelity: Review must be provable at machine resolution when systems operate at machine speed.
  • Observer independence: Verification cannot require permission from the system operator.
  • Distributed inspection: Centralized logging alone does not satisfy the Act.
Closing Doctrine: TRUST REQUIRES REPLAY. MEMORY MUST BE PUBLIC. DRIFT MUST BE DISCLOSED. AUTOMATION MUST REMAIN AUDITABLE.
