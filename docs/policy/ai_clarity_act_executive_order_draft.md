# THE AI CLARITY ACT

*Executive Order on Replayability, Provenance, and Audit Standards for Public-Facing AI*

## Constitutional Authority

By the authority vested in the President by Article II of the Constitution, and to secure the guarantees of due process under the Fifth Amendment and equal protection under the Fourteenth Amendment, it is ordered:

Automated systems that determine or materially influence public outcomes shall not operate beyond meaningful review. Liberty and property interests protected by the Constitution cannot be delegated to uninspectable processes.

## Section 1. Purpose

To establish minimum replayability, provenance, and audit standards for high-impact artificial intelligence systems operating within public institutions, regulated sectors, or federally contracted environments.

## Section 2. Foundational Principle

No automated system exercising influence over public outcomes should operate as an uninspectable black box when its outputs materially affect human rights, economic opportunity, legal process, or public trust.

## Section 3. Constitutional Rules

### 1. Replay Requirement

Any materially consequential AI output must preserve a replay path sufficient for independent review.

Minimum replay surface:

- model identifier
- policy/version identifier
- timestamp
- input provenance classification
- transformation chain
- output receipt hash

### 2. Drift Disclosure

If model behavior materially changes after deployment:

- the drift event must be logged
- prior outputs remain historically preserved
- affected systems enter REVIEW_REQUIRED state

Silent model replacement is prohibited in covered systems.

### 3. Public Audit Memory

Covered systems must maintain append-only audit logs for:

- policy changes
- model upgrades
- safety overrides
- replay failures
- major drift events

Audit history may not be silently rewritten.

### 4. Human Review Preservation

No AI confidence score may be treated as final authority.

```txt
A SCORE IS NOT A VERDICT.
```

Human appeal pathways must exist for:

- employment
- benefits
- housing
- credit
- legal recommendation systems
- public-sector eligibility systems

### 5. Multi-Observer Verification

High-impact systems must support independent verification across multiple observers or environments.

```txt
CONSENSUS ≠ CONVERGENCE
```

Agreement without reproducible replay is insufficient.

### 6. Allegation Boundary

Systems may classify evidence states.

Systems may not infer criminality, motive, or intent without human adjudication.

## Section 4. Enforcement Categories

Agencies shall publish category designations for all covered systems within 90 days.

## Section 5. Closing Doctrine

```txt
TRUST REQUIRES REPLAY.
MEMORY MUST BE PUBLIC.
DRIFT MUST BE DISCLOSED.
AUTOMATION MUST REMAIN AUDITABLE.
```
