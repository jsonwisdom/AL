## SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE

This Act may be cited as the **Behavioral Systems Accountability Act of 2027**.

## SECTION 2. CONGRESSIONAL FINDINGS AND PURPOSE

Congress finds that:

1. Certain large-scale digital platforms deploy algorithmic systems that optimize user attention, engagement, retention, and behavioral return frequency.
2. When such systems are deployed at industrial scale and directed toward minors, they may create distinct developmental, privacy, and consumer-protection risks.
3. The purpose of this Act is to establish narrow, mechanics-focused accountability obligations for covered behavioral optimization systems involving minors.
4. Nothing in this Act authorizes federal or state officials to direct, privilege, suppress, rank, or disfavor lawful speech based on viewpoint, ideology, political position, or narrative content.
5. This Act regulates the design, operation, documentation, and accountability surfaces of covered behavioral optimization systems, not the ideas, viewpoints, or lawful expression carried by such systems.
6. Open, interoperable, privacy-preserving compliance standards are necessary to prevent safety obligations from becoming proprietary barriers to entry or centralized identity infrastructure.
7. Covered platforms should be incentivized to produce verifiable records, signed mitigation receipts, and independent replay surfaces that allow accountability without mass surveillance.
8. The public interest is served by preserving innovation, competition, privacy, anonymous speech, and constitutional neutrality while requiring industrial-scale systems to account for measurable behavioral optimization practices affecting minors.

**Purpose.** The purpose of this Act is to create a narrow federal framework for verified mechanics compliance by covered platforms, including scaled obligations, privacy-preserving eligibility tools, replayable accountability surfaces, open schemas, user portability, and safe-harbor incentives.

## SECTION 3. DEFINITIONS

(a) **Behavioral Optimization System** means any algorithmic process that ranks, recommends, sequences, amplifies, or personalizes user-facing content based in whole or substantial part on predicted engagement, retention, session duration, return frequency, sharing, or other behavioral metrics.

(b) **Compliance Stack** means the technical specification `CONSTITUTIONAL_COMPLIANCE_STACK_V0.1`, or successor version maintained under public rulemaking, including its Layers A–G and associated schemas and receipt formats.

(c) **Covered Platform** has the meaning given in Section 4.

(d) **Material Mitigation** means any platform change intended to address compulsive use, developmental risk, or behavioral escalation patterns affecting minors.

(e) **Minor** means an individual under 18 years of age.

(f) **Independent Replay Surface** means a publicly documented, cryptographically verifiable interface allowing qualified auditors to confirm policy declarations, mitigation chronology, and compliance integrity without access to private user data, personal feeds, or raw content.

(g) **Open Compliance Schema** means vendor-neutral, publicly available JSON schemas, event vocabularies, and signature formats published under this Act.

(h) **Viewpoint Discrimination** includes any requirement or incentive to privilege, suppress, or re-rank content based on ideology, political position, narrative framing, or subjective harmfulness of lawful ideas.

(i) **Qualified Auditor** means independent researchers, nonprofits, or technical experts designated by the Commission under transparent criteria focused on expertise and conflict-of-interest safeguards.

## SECTION 4. COVERED SYSTEMS AND SCALED THRESHOLDS

(a) **Covered Platform.** A platform is covered under this Act only if it meets all of the following criteria:

1. Exceeds a defined monthly active user threshold in the United States, as established by the Commission through public rulemaking;
2. Knowingly serves or reasonably expects to serve minors;
3. Uses a Behavioral Optimization System to rank, recommend, sequence, or amplify user-facing content;
4. Optimizes for behavioral metrics including engagement, retention, session duration, return frequency, sharing, or monetized attention.

(b) **Enhanced Obligations.** Enhanced obligations under this Act apply where a covered platform also meets one or more risk-amplifying factors:

1. Substantial minor user base, as a percentage of total users;
2. High average daily use among minors;
3. Personalized ranking based on behavioral history or inferred user state;
4. Use of autoplay, infinite scroll, streaks, variable-reward loops, or similar compulsive design patterns;
5. Material evidence of compulsive-use or developmental-risk patterns in internal or public data.

(c) **Exemptions.** The following are exempt unless they intentionally deploy behavioral optimization systems at covered scale:

1. Small platforms below the Commission’s thresholds;
2. Open-source projects without commercial behavioral optimization;
3. Chronological-only services with no algorithmic personalization or amplification;
4. Private messaging services without algorithmic public-feed ranking;
5. Educational, nonprofit, or research systems with limited distribution and no monetized attention model.

(d) **No Evasion.** A platform may not avoid coverage by fragmenting services, subsidiaries, domains, apps, or recommendation surfaces where they function as an integrated behavioral optimization system.

(e) **Periodic Review.** The Commission shall review and adjust thresholds and risk factors every three years through public notice-and-comment rulemaking, taking into account changes in technology, user behavior, and market structure.

## SECTION 5. MINOR SAFETY OBLIGATIONS — MECHANICS ONLY

Covered platforms shall implement mechanics-focused safety obligations for minors, including:

1. Default reduced-personalization or chronological modes;
2. Autoplay disable capability with user control;
3. Infinite-scroll interruption mechanisms;
4. Nighttime usage friction and time-awareness prompts;
5. Guardian visibility and control tools.

Obligations under this section are limited to system mechanics and shall not extend to content moderation, viewpoint classification, or editorial decisions.

## SECTION 6. TRANSPARENCY AND REPLAYABLE ACCOUNTABILITY

(a) Covered platforms shall maintain public Optimization Disclosure records per Compliance Stack Layer B.

(b) Every material mitigation or safety intervention shall emit a signed Mitigation Receipt per Compliance Stack Layer C.

(c) Covered platforms shall provide Independent Replay Surfaces per Compliance Stack Layer D, allowing qualified third parties to verify chronology and integrity without access to private user data.

(d) All disclosures shall conform to the Open Compliance Schema per Compliance Stack Layer E.

## SECTION 7. AGE ELIGIBILITY INFRASTRUCTURE

(a) Platforms may satisfy age-related obligations through privacy-preserving Zero-Knowledge Age Range Proofs per Compliance Stack Layer A.

(b) Such proofs shall be limited to age-range attestation only.

(c) No permanent identity custody, government identification storage, or linkable identifier shall be required under this Act.

(d) Privacy-preserving credentials, anonymous credentials, device-local attestations, and ephemeral verification tokens are encouraged.

## SECTION 8. SAFE HARBOR FOR VERIFIED MECHANICS COMPLIANCE

(a) **Presumptive Safe Harbor.** A covered platform shall receive presumptive safe harbor from civil liability arising from design-based claims related to minor behavioral optimization if the platform demonstrates good-faith compliance with Sections 5, 6, 7, and 9.

(b) **Required Showing.** To qualify, a platform must maintain:

1. Public Optimization Disclosure records;
2. Signed Mitigation Receipts for all material interventions;
3. Independent Replay Surfaces accessible to qualified auditors;
4. Open Compliance Schema compatibility;
5. Privacy-preserving age eligibility infrastructure where invoked;
6. User portability mechanisms;
7. Adversarial research access procedures.

(c) **No Safe Harbor for Knowing Misrepresentation.** Safe harbor shall not apply where a platform knowingly falsifies, withholds, or materially misrepresents required compliance records or receipts.

(d) **No Editorial Condition.** Safe harbor may not be conditioned on adoption of any viewpoint, political balancing rule, ideological ranking preference, narrative priority, or government-directed content outcome.

(e) **Revocation and Cure.** If a platform fails compliance review, the Commission shall provide written notice and a reasonable cure period of not less than 90 days unless the violation involves knowing falsification or substantial risk of imminent harm to minors.

(f) **Small Platform Protection.** Platforms below covered-system thresholds, or those operating in good faith with limited resources, shall not be denied safe harbor solely for lacking enterprise-grade audit infrastructure, provided they meet scaled, proportionate obligations under the Compliance Stack.

## SECTION 9. INTEROPERABILITY AND ANTI-CAPTURE

(a) Users shall have the right to export and import moderation preferences, trust settings, safety filters, social graphs, and recommendation histories.

(b) Compliance tooling must use open, vendor-neutral schemas. Proprietary-only solutions do not satisfy safe-harbor requirements.

(c) The Commission shall maintain open schema references and shall not require use of any exclusive vendor, private identity provider, or proprietary audit system.

## SECTION 10. FIRST AMENDMENT FIREWALL

Nothing in this Act shall be construed to:

1. Require or incentivize viewpoint discrimination, ideological balancing, political weighting, or government-directed ranking outcomes;
2. Authorize compelled editorial decisions;
3. Require disclosure of private user content, private communications, or raw feeds;
4. Permit government officials to direct, privilege, suppress, rank, or disfavor lawful speech based on viewpoint, ideology, political position, or narrative content.

## SECTION 11. ENFORCEMENT AND PREEMPTION

(a) **Primary Federal Enforcement.** The Federal Trade Commission shall have primary authority to enforce this Act, treating violations as unfair or deceptive acts or practices under section 5 of the Federal Trade Commission Act, 15 U.S.C. § 45.

(b) **State Attorney General Enforcement.** A State attorney general may bring a civil action on behalf of its residents, provided that:

1. Prior written notice is given to the Commission;
2. The action enforces this Act and does not impose conflicting behavioral optimization obligations;
3. Remedies do not require viewpoint discrimination, ideological ranking, or government-directed content outcomes.

(c) **Federal Preemption.** This Act preempts any state law, regulation, or remedy that imposes conflicting or additional obligations on covered platforms regarding:

1. Age eligibility infrastructure;
2. Recommender-system documentation and optimization disclosures;
3. Mitigation receipts and replay surfaces;
4. Open compliance schemas;
5. Behavioral optimization design obligations involving minors.

(d) **Savings Clause.** Nothing in this Act preempts generally applicable state laws addressing fraud, consumer protection, privacy, contract, or child sexual exploitation, provided such laws do not impose inconsistent mechanics-based compliance requirements on covered platforms.

(e) **Limited Private Right of Action.** A private right of action exists only for knowing and material failure to maintain required compliance records, falsification of signed receipts, or denial of legally required portability rights. No private action may seek to compel changes to content ranking or moderation policies.

(f) **Remedies.** Available remedies include civil penalties, injunctive relief limited to compliance with this Act, mandated cure plans, independent audits, and temporary safe-harbor suspension. Remedies shall not compel viewpoint-based ranking, disclosure of private user content, or architectural changes beyond verifiable mechanics compliance.

## SECTION 12. RULEMAKING AND REVIEW

(a) The Commission shall issue implementing rules within 18 months of enactment, including precise thresholds, Compliance Stack updates, qualified auditor criteria, and schema maintenance procedures.

(b) Thresholds and risk factors shall be reviewed every three years through public notice-and-comment rulemaking.

(c) Any successor version of the Compliance Stack must preserve the core invariants of mechanics-only regulation, privacy-preserving eligibility, open schemas, independent replayability, and First Amendment neutrality.

## SECTION 13. EFFECTIVE DATE

This Act takes effect 180 days after enactment, except for rulemaking provisions which take effect immediately.
