## I. Purpose

This surface defines how courtroom verdicts map into institutional posture, not into truth, policy, or authority.

A courtroom verdict may:

- constrain institutional action,
- block institutional action,
- require disclosure of taint,
- route jurisdiction,
- or terminate proceedings.

A courtroom verdict may not:

- command reality,
- create truth,
- manufacture authority,
- repair upstream defects,
- or generate policy.

This surface prevents the corruption vector:

```text
admissibility classification -> real-world authority inflation
```

## II. Core Doctrine

### 1. Consequences Are Downstream-Only

Institutional posture MUST follow courtroom verdicts.

It may not reinterpret, soften, or override them.

### 2. Consequences Are Not Truth

No consequence implies correctness, reality alignment, or epistemic authority.

### 3. Consequences Are Not Policy

Institutional posture is a constraint, not a directive.

### 4. Consequences Cannot Repair Upstream Defects

No verdict can:

- fix reconstructability,
- repair sealing integrity,
- cleanse taint,
- override refusal,
- or fabricate continuity.

### 5. Consequences Must Preserve Constitutional Integrity

If a verdict blocks action, institutions MUST stop.
