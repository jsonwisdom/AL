## Origin

This doctrine interprets evolutionary quantum feature-map search as a governance problem rather than a speed claim. A quantum kernel defines a geometry of similarity; a genetic algorithm searches candidate geometries; a constitutional receipt system determines when a candidate may operate, adapt, abstain, or terminate.

The source research is *Flexible Genetic Algorithm for Quantum Support Vector Machines* (Nguyen et al., accepted manuscript, 2026). The research motivates the search architecture; the jurisprudence below is an independent systems interpretation.

## Governing Principle

> The executor is mortal. The charter is amendable. The geometry is replaceable. The receipt is append-only.

An output without a receipt is an unaccountable assertion. A refusal with a receipt may still be legitimate.

## Constitutional Registry

The ratified registry is:

```text
R = {B, Phi, Delta, T, W, M, X, Y}
```

Where:

- `B` — approved circuit families
- `Phi` — approved embedding strategies or preambles
- `Delta` — approved drift tolerances
- `T` — performance and stress floors
- `W` — monitoring-window definitions
- `M` — persistence and re-entry requirements
- `X` — shadow charter: scope limits, assumptions, exclusions, and declared ignorance
- `Y` — fiscal charter: compute, monitoring, triage, human-review, reserve, and sunset budgets

The registry must be versioned, timestamped, hashed, and ratified before evaluation. Post-result threshold selection is prohibited.

## Three-Tier Amendment Hierarchy

| Layer | Component | Amendment rule |
|---|---|---|
| Preamble | `U_phi(x)` embedding and claim formation | Full constitutional convention |
| Constitution | `E`, the entanglement or CX topology | Bounded structural re-entry |
| Bylaws | `theta`, local rotation parameters | Continuous local adjustment |

Escalation order:

```text
Tune theta -> Review E -> Reconsider Phi
```

Every escalation requires a receipt showing that the lower-level remedy was attempted and failed.

## Pareto Legitimacy

The search must preserve non-dominated circuits rather than hide policy choices inside one weighted score.

A circuit survives when no alternative is at least as good in clean accuracy and stress accuracy, no more expensive, and no less stable, with at least one strict improvement.

The machine exposes the feasible frontier. Human authority selects the mission trade-off.

## Separation of Powers

Ratification is bicameral:

- **Architect** — certifies technical consistency, statistical validity, and frontier integrity.
- **Operator** — certifies mission fit, external risk tolerance, and operational consequences.

Both sign the exact registry hash before dataset evaluation. Dissent is appended as a public receipt; it is not silently overridden.

## Witness Architecture

Independent monitors include:

- `s_f` — full live stream
- `s_h` — isolated, time-lagged holdout
- `s_r` — pre-ratified red-team perturbation stream
- `s_p` — physical substrate fidelity
- `s_phi` — kernel entropy or eigenspace shock
- `s_e` — remaining fiscal capacity

Witness independence requires separate provenance, hashes, storage, sampling rules, and statistical-dependence checks. Multiple outputs from one corrupted pipeline do not constitute multiple witnesses.

## Crisis Attribution

```text
Data distress + healthy substrate -> governance review
Substrate distress -> recalibration hold
Entropy/eigenspace shock -> human triage buffer
Scope mismatch -> refusal receipt
Insufficient budget -> risk-tiered fallback or abstention
```

Hardware failure may not indict the constitution while failing to execute it faithfully.

## Shadow Charter and Declared Ignorance

`X` records the conditions under which the system renounces jurisdiction, including:

- supported data domains
- stationarity assumptions
- adversarial bounds
- hardware-fidelity floors
- known blind spots
- excluded uses
- unresolved uncertainties

When the domain violates the declared scope, the required output is:

```text
NO_VALID_CONSTITUTION
DECLARED_SCOPE_MISMATCH
```

The refusal must be signed and replayable.

## Rights and Fiscal Floor

A proposed fiscal charter is invalid before deployment when its austerity mode would exceed the ratified rights-risk ceiling.

The Treasury has two lawful choices:

```text
Fund the rights floor
OR
Narrow the jurisdiction
```

It may not underfund a high-impact system and quietly lower its truth or rights standards.

## Sunset and Veto Window

Budget exhaustion triggers a public fatal-budget receipt and a bounded veto window.

A valid veto must include:

- reference to the fatal receipt
- required signatures
- committed funding sufficient for the extension
- rights compliance during the extension
- fixed expiration
- remediation plan

A veto is one-shot and non-renewable without full re-ratification.

If no valid veto arrives, the system executes pre-authorized demobilization:

1. seal the charter, frontier, last known good kernel, and crisis dossier;
2. sign the terminal internal state;
3. revoke runtime keys, credentials, and write authority;
4. hand off to the ratified fallback process;
5. anchor the final state in write-once storage.

The system dies operationally, not historically.

## Successor Doctrine

A successor is a new constitutional birth, not a restart.

It requires:

```text
New charter
+ New budget
+ New keys
+ Fresh scope check
+ Fresh substrate validation
+ Independent ratification
```

A successor may inherit archives as evidence, but never authority. Later discoveries append posthumous correction receipts without modifying the predecessor's sealed state.

## Receipt Chain

Canonical receipt classes:

- `rho_R` — charter ratification
- `rho_operation` — exercised authority
- `rho_refusal` — jurisdictional abstention
- `rho_crisis` — documented confusion
- `rho_fiscal` — budget depletion
- `rho_veto` — proof-of-cure extension
- `rho_sunset` — terminal internal state
- `rho_authority` — external demobilization authorization
- `rho_posthumous` — later historical correction

The archive may migrate containers and cryptographic algorithms, but each migration must verify the old chain, preserve the original records, and append a new signed migration receipt.

## Supreme Archival Law

> No executor may erase the evidence of its authority. No successor may inherit authority from historical compatibility. No archival migration may alter the original event. Every correction appends; none overwrites.

The hierarchy is:

```text
Executor < Charter < Rights < Receipt Chain
```

The receipt chain is supreme as evidence, not as moral truth. It preserves enough provenance for future auditors to determine what happened, what was believed, what was permitted, and where the system failed.
