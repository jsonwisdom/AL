# Civic Kernel Guide Template

## 1. What this guide is
Explain the guide's purpose in plain language.

## 2. Why it matters
Explain the practical citizen-facing problem this guide helps route.

## 3. Core concept distinctions
Define the key distinctions that prevent wrong-pipeline action.

Examples:
- notice is not a final order
- application is not approval
- complaint is not automatic enforcement
- disclosure is not wrongdoing

## 4. Who controls what
Identify the authority surface.

Separate:
- federal authority
- state authority
- county or local authority
- court or administrative body
- platform or portal operator

## 5. How to apply or start
Give the first practical steps.

Use official portals and official contact paths only.

## 6. Critical deadlines and failure prevention
Bold important deadlines.

Explain what commonly causes loss of process, delay, or abandonment.

## 7. Key warnings
Add stress-state warnings that prevent panic inference or wrong routing.

Use short declarative stabilizers where appropriate.

## 8. Appeals and escalation
Explain escalation paths without promising outcomes.

Separate informal complaint, administrative appeal, court process, and emergency routing where relevant.

## 9. Required documents
Use checkboxes.

- [ ] Provide only documents requested
- [ ] Keep copies/screenshots with dates
- [ ] Save notices, confirmations, and correspondence

## 10. Where to get help
List official sources first.

Include 211 where crisis routing is relevant.

## 11. What this guide is not
This guide explains process only.

It is not legal advice, eligibility determination, medical advice, financial advice, or an outcome guarantee.

Eligibility, enforcement, benefits, orders, and decisions are determined by the relevant agency, court, board, or official authority — not this guide.

## Kernel Guardrails

A guide may reuse structure.
It may never reuse state-specific legal conclusions without re-verification.

Template does not equal facts.
Facts do not equal guidance.
Guidance does not equal legal conclusion.

## Stress-First Formatting Rules

- Keep sections short and mobile-readable
- Prefer checklists over paragraphs under stress conditions
- Bold deadlines and critical warnings
- Cross-link instead of repeating long explanations
- Use official sources only
- Do not use news, blogs, or social media as primary authority

## Operational Grammar

identify authority → identify process → preserve evidence → respond before deadline → escalate correctly → use official sources
